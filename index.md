## The Core Idea

Independent retailers of THCA hemp products — smoke shops, CBD stores, wellness retailers, convenience operators — each buy too little volume to command real wholesale pricing. A distributor looks at a single store ordering $2,000/month and prices accordingly. The same distributor looking at forty stores committing $80,000/month through one negotiated contract prices very differently.

The consortium is the mechanism that turns many small buyers into one large buyer — **without** any individual member having to buy in bulk quantities themselves.

## Why an Organization, Not a Spreadsheet

The obvious lightweight alternative — a shared monthly spreadsheet of "preferred vendors and current pricing" — fails for structural reasons explored in depth on the [trade-offs page]({{ '/strengths-weaknesses' | relative_url }}). In short:

- A spreadsheet has no **enforceable contracts** behind its prices
- No **volume commitments**, so distributors price it like walk-in traffic
- No **audit rights**, so nobody knows if quoted prices are actually honored
- No **continuity** — it dies when the person maintaining it loses interest
- In this market specifically (THCA hemp), extreme regulatory and supply flux makes informal arrangements collapse fastest

A century of group-purchasing history across healthcare, hardware, grocery, and foodservice converges on the same answer: aggregation only sustains when there's a real entity with governance, contracts, and accountability.
