# Membership Agreement — Working Skeleton

A draft structure for the member-side agreement. Every bracketed item is a team decision; every section marked **[COUNSEL]** needs attorney review before use. This is a design artifact to brief counsel from, not a signable document.

---

## Article I — Definitions & Parties

- **Organization** — [entity name], a [PA LLC / PA nonprofit corporation] (see [Entity Structure]({{ '/entity-structure' | relative_url }}))
- **Member** — a business entity (not individual) operating a retail location within [PA / NJ / service territory]
- **Covered Categories** — THCA hemp flower, pre-rolls, vapes, concentrates, edibles, and [accessories? packaging?] as enumerated in Schedule A
- **Consortium Contract** — a written supply agreement between the Organization and a Vendor covering one or more Covered Categories
- **Purchase Data** — records of Member purchases under Consortium Contracts

## Article II — Eligibility & Admission

1. Applicant must hold all licenses required for its retail operations in its jurisdiction
2. Applicant attests products purchased under Consortium Contracts will be sold only where lawful
3. **[DECISION]** Age-gate attestation (21+ retail) as membership condition?
4. Organization may decline or terminate for cause; termination-for-cause procedure with notice + cure period
5. Founding Member class: closed after [date / N members]; privileges per Schedule B

## Article III — Term, Renewal, Exit

| Provision | Working position |
|---|---|
| Initial term | 12 months, auto-renewing annually |
| Voluntary exit | 60 days written notice; exit effective at end of current quarter |
| Immediate exit rights | Organization's material breach; loss of vendor licensure by Organization |
| Expulsion | Supermajority board vote for fraud or material violation, with cure period first |
| Effect of exit | Member loses pricing/rebates immediately; accrued but unpaid rebates paid out on next quarterly cycle |

## Article IV — Purchasing Commitments

This is the article that makes the model real. Tiered obligations:

1. **Member tier:** no volume commitment. Access to standard consortium pricing.
2. **Committed tier:** quarterly minimum purchase of $[X] across Covered Categories, measured on Purchase Data reconciliation. Shortfalls: [grace quarter / tier downgrade / fee].
3. **Exclusivity:** NONE beyond Covered Categories — members retain full off-contract purchasing rights. (Voluntary participation is both legally safer and historically standard; see [Consortium Model]({{ '/consortium-model' | relative_url }}).)
4. True-up mechanics: quarterly reconciliation; committed-tier shortfalls roll one quarter.

## Article V — Pricing, Fees & Rebates

1. Consortium Contract pricing is confidential; Member may share internally and with advisors under confidentiality
2. Vendor administrative fees, if charged, are disclosed annually in writing, capped at [3%], per the transparency practice established in healthcare GPO regulation ([precedents]({{ '/case-studies' | relative_url }}))
3. Dues: $[X] annually for Member tier; waived/discounted for Committed and Founding tiers — **[DECISION]** final fee schedule
4. Rebates collected by Organization are passed through quarterly via ACH less [stated] administrative retention
5. Annual audited savings statement delivered to each Committed-tier member (the anti-"overhead tax" commitment from the [trade-offs analysis]({{ '/strengths-weaknesses' | relative_url }}))

## Article VI — Purchase Data & Privacy

1. Member grants Organization right to obtain Purchase Data from Vendors for reconciliation, audit, rebate administration, and negotiation analytics
2. Aggregated, de-identified data may be used for benchmarking and published market reports
3. Individual Member data is not sold or shared outside audit/administration purposes
4. Data retention [X years]; destruction on exit

## Article VII — Governance

1. One vote per Member location; Committed and Founding tiers weighted [1x / 2x / 3x] — **[DECISION]** weighting
2. Board: [5–7] seats; Founding Members eligible; staggered terms; elected annually
3. Product Committee: [3–5] member representatives control catalog awards (the healthcare-GPO lesson: users govern the catalog, negotiators execute)
4. Conflict-of-interest policy: board members' businesses may serve as Vendors only through arm's-length competitive award, disclosed and recused
5. **Operator neutrality (mandatory):** the Administrator/Operator may hold no ownership, employment, or revenue interest in any Member or Vendor business; compensation keyed solely to member-outcome metrics ([rationale]({{ '/operator-role' | relative_url }}))
6. Amendments: ordinary by majority; pricing/fee changes by [supermajority] with 30-day notice

## Article VIII — Compliance & Regulatory

1. Member warrants lawful resale conduct in its jurisdiction(s)
2. Organization makes no warranty of product legality in any particular jurisdiction — each Member responsible for its own state-law compliance (critical given state-by-state divergence in intoxicating-hemp rules)
3. Vendor qualification standards set by Product Committee: DEA-registered lab COAs, delta-9 ≤0.3% dry weight, contaminant panels, producer/processor licensure, product liability insurance minimums
4. Recourse terms for failed/non-compliant batches flow through from vendor contracts
5. Both parties cooperate on any regulatory inquiry; Organization maintains compliance file per Consortium Contract

## Article IX — Antitrust Compliance

The whole-group exposure area. **[COUNSEL] mandatory.**

1. Member acknowledges Organization conducts all negotiations independently; Members do not participate in price discussions with other Members regarding vendors
2. Prohibited: agreements among Members to boycott any vendor, allocate territories/customers, or fix resale prices
3. Organization maintains written antitrust policy; annual review by counsel (the Capper-Volstead mushroom-co-op failure shows how expensive improvised coordination gets)
4. Violation is grounds for immediate expulsion

## Article X — Dispute Resolution & Miscellaneous

1. Governing law: Pennsylvania
2. Disputes: escalation → mediation → binding arbitration [county]; injunctive relief carve-out for confidentiality/data breaches
3. Notices; assignment prohibited without consent; entire agreement; severability
4. Force majeure including change in law rendering Covered Categories unlawful — with wind-down provisions (asset return, data destruction, rebate settlement) pre-agreed now rather than improvised later
