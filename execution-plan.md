# Execution Plan — New Hope Pilot

The team decision: **Option C hybrid confirmed, PA-only launch anchored around New Hope / Bucks County**, with expansion path toward Greater Philly and eventually tristate as law permits. This page is the working schedule.

---

## Why This Shape Works

**New Hope geography is an asset, not a limitation.** NJ's April 2026 ban on THCA flower pushed demand across the Delaware — PA border-town shops are absorbing displaced NJ customers right now. Every prospective member near New Hope has *recently experienced* demand growth and regulatory whiplash, which makes them maximally receptive to two pitches: better pricing on surging volume, and price/order auditing they've never had.

**The founding group maps cleanly onto the structure:**

| Role | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Founding entity partners | The four partners | Formation capital, governance, vendor relationships |
| Tier-1 seed members | 4 shop owners ("the five") | Anchor volume for first RFQ |
| Operator candidate | One current member | Pivots to vendor-neutral liaison: software, logistics, reconciliation — see neutrality requirements below |

## Solving "I Don't Get How This Helps Me"

The Tier-1 prospects' stated confusion dissolves against their actual pain: **none of them can audit their own orders or pricing today.** They buy at whatever the rep quotes, with no baseline, no comparison, no recourse.

The pitch sequence for them isn't discounts-first. It's:

1. **Month 1 — you get your eyes opened:** we collect your last 3 months of invoices (redacted) and show you exactly what you paid vs market. Even with zero negotiating, every owner sees their own pricing drift and overcharges in black and white.
2. **Month 2 — you get the group contract:** the five of us together commit ~$10–12K/month. That number changes what vendors will quote us. You keep ordering exactly like always; the price sheet underneath you changes.
3. **Month 3+ — you get enforcement:** monthly reconciliation catches any invoice above contract price and gets it credited back automatically. Nobody has ever done this for these shops. Once seen, the spreadsheet alternative is obviously dead.

And the intuition they'll each arrive at — "whoever buys the most gets the best discounts, and eventually we're all there" — is precisely the tier-ladder design from the [model]({{ '/consortium-model' | relative_url }}): retroactive tiers mean every new member retroactively improves everyone's effective pricing. Their instinct is correct; the consortium is just the machine that makes it real and enforceable.

## The Operator Candidate

One of the five is a natural fit for the vendor-neutral liaison role: software and logistics orientation, wants to solve shipping/distribution friction for everyone. That's the [Operator role]({{ '/operator-role' | relative_url }}) almost verbatim.

Transition requirements (from the neutrality doctrine):

- They cannot simultaneously be the referee and a player. Path: wind down their retail buying through the consortium over one quarter, OR formally split — retail interest held passively/transferred, with the conflict policy applied as if a stranger held the job
- Compensated going forward on member-outcome metrics (audited savings delivered, retention) — never purchase volume
- In the interim, they can run the software/logistics workstream immediately (invoice collection, reconciliation tooling, delivery consolidation) since those tasks touch all parties equally while contracts aren't yet signed

Their logistics/consolidation idea deserves its own workstream: pooled delivery scheduling across New Hope-area shops (one truck run serving multiple members) is a second efficiency dividend on top of pricing — historically the co-op warehouse function from the Ace/True Value model, achievable later at micro-scale.

## Decision Gates

1. **Week 3 gate:** if baseline analysis shows members already buy within ~5% of best market pricing, aggregation value is thin → pivot category discussion before spending on RFQ
2. **Week 5 gate:** if best RFQ lands <8% below baseline → falsification criterion hit; stop, reassess category or structure
3. **Nov 12 federal gate:** whatever the pilot shows, Schedule A gets reviewed against the total-THC redefinition; pivot categories (packaging, glass, POS/compliance services, compliant CBD) pre-planned so the org survives the product shift
