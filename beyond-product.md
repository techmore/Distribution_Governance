# Beyond Product: The Services Layer

Product purchasing (flower, vapes, edibles) is the wedge, not the ceiling. Every mature consortium in the [historical record]({{ '/case-studies' | relative_url }}) expanded from price aggregation into services aggregation — and the service categories are where the recurring-revenue and stickiness live. This page maps the full stack.

This page splits into two halves: **services members buy** (we aggregate demand and negotiate) and **service vendors who pay** (they want access to our consolidated member base). Same mechanism, both directions.

---

## Tier 1 — Payment & Financial Services

The highest-value aggregation category after product itself, because payment costs are a pure margin leak for every shop:

| Service | The member pain today | What consortium negotiation changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Merchant services / card processing** | Small shops pay 3–3.5% + fees because individually they're tiny accounts; cannabis-adjacent MCC codes add risk premiums | Pooled processing volume (~$150K/mo at 30 shops, mostly cash-adjacent) justifies custom rates; ISOs actively compete for consolidated portfolios |
| **Cash management & vault** | Cash-heavy category means armored pickup or risky self-handling | Group contract with a cash-logistics provider serving all locations on one route |
| **Banking relationships** | Each shop navigates CRB-friendly banks alone ([see banking page]({{ '/banking-operations' | relative_url }})) | Consortium negotiates as one commercial relationship; members get pre-vetted account packages |
| **Insurance (product liability, general)** | Tiny shops pay tiny-policy premiums with terrible terms | Group program quoting across the membership base |

Payment processing is the proven playbook here: restaurant GPOs negotiate processing rates alongside food rebates, and it's often their second-biggest savings line. An ISO offering "portfolio pricing" across our whole membership earns volume they could never assemble shop-by-shop — which is why *they'll pay for access too* (below).

## Tier 2 — Information Systems

Your operator candidate's home territory. Software is where small retailers overpay most per unit of value:

- **POS systems** — every shop independently buys, implements, and misconfigures age-verification/compliance features. One vetted configuration + group license rate beats five solo decisions. POS vendors offer meaningful portfolio discounts and will co-fund rollout.
- **Inventory/purchasing software** — the reconciliation loop ([mechanics]({{ '/consortium-model' | relative_url }})) needs member-side purchase data anyway; negotiating a group tool that feeds it standardizes our own data pipeline
- **Compliance/document management** — COA storage, batch tracking, audit prep: currently shoebox-grade at most shops; a group subscription turns our biggest operational headache into a solved utility
- **Age verification / ID scanning hardware** — commodity purchase, obvious pooling

The IS layer compounds: once members run common systems, the consortium's data quality improves, which strengthens both negotiation analytics and vendor audit — a flywheel the spreadsheet model can never build.

## Tier 3 — Policies, Procedures & Compliance Infrastructure

Not purchasable from one vendor — but aggregatable as *shared professional services*:

- **Standard operating procedures library** — age-check protocols, refusal logs, incident response, recall procedures: written once by counsel/consultants, adopted by all members. Individual shops would never fund this; split thirty ways it's trivial.
- **Compliance training program** — onboarding curriculum for shop staff, refreshed when regulations shift (which in this market is constantly — see [state law matrix]({{ '/state-law-matrix' | relative_url }}))
- **Legal retainer sharing** — regulatory monitoring and template work split across the base; each shop gets counsel-grade coverage at co-op prices
- **Accounting/tax standards** — 280E-adjacent bookkeeping practices standardized across members

## Tier 4 — Community Support & Mutual Aid

The softest tier but strategically real — it's what makes membership feel like belonging rather than a discount club:

- **Regulatory watch & alerts** — the consortium monitors PA/NJ/federal developments once and briefs all members; during the Nov 2026 federal transition this becomes mission-critical
- **Group advocacy voice** — a consortium speaking for 40+ licensed businesses carries weight in Harrisburg that no solo shop does (the 501(c)(6) [trade association form]({{ '/entity-structure' | relative_url }}) exists precisely for this)
- **Peer forums & best-practice exchange** — merchandising, staffing, security: owners solving shared problems together
- **Crisis mutual aid** — coordinated response when a member faces a licensing issue, robbery, or supply shock

## How Service Vendors Pay to Participate

Same logic as product distributors, stronger economics — service providers have near-zero marginal cost per added account, so paying the consortium for qualified access pencils out fast:

| Model | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Referral/admin fee** | Vendor pays consortium % of member billings (mirrors healthcare GPO admin-fee structure) |
| **Sponsorship** | Flat annual fee for "preferred vendor" status — always awarded through committee review, never sold outright (vendor-capture protection) |
| **RFP-for-services** | Members' pooled demand goes to competitive bid exactly like product: merchant services, insurance, POS each run an annual RFQ |
| **Revenue share on negotiated rates** | Consortium retains small slice of achieved discount; members still net clear savings |

Guardrail from the [trade-offs analysis]({{ '/strengths-weaknesses' | relative_url }}): service-vendor money must flow through the same disclosure discipline as product admin fees, or member trust dies identically.

## Sequencing

Do not launch with all four tiers. Product first (the pilot), then — roughly in order of member pain × vendor willingness-to-pay:

1. **Merchant services** (biggest leak, eager vendors) — target around month 4–6
2. **IS/POS group program** (operator's strength) — month 6–9
3. **Policies/procedures library** (counsel-dependent) — as formation work completes
4. **Community layer** — grows organically once monthly meetings exist

Each addition deepens member stickiness: a shop getting product pricing + better card rates + POS support + compliance training has four reasons not to churn, and none of them are sentimental.
